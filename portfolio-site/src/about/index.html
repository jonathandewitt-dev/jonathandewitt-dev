Who I am

I tend to question abstractions instead of accepting them. If something feels off, I dig into it, sometimes to the point of rebuilding parts of the stack just to understand the tradeoffs. That's led me through all the major UI frameworks, each time reevaluating what actually holds up under real use.

I care a lot about long-term maintainability, mental models, and avoiding unnecessary complexity. I'm less interested in following patterns because they're popular, and more interested in whether they're actually the best direction in practice.

I find creative fulfillment in turning accepted approaches on their head and engineering completely unexplored ideas. That curiosity leads me down rabbit holes that occasionally end with me building another solution to solve whatever problem I found.

Who I know

I try to stay involved in the industry beyond my day job. A lot of that comes down to building relationships and actually talking to the people shaping the space.

Jon Kern, co-author of the Agile Manifesto, is a friend and mentor of mine. He's had a huge influence on how I think about engineering, teams, and the work itself.

I've also had the privilege of exchanging ideas with other co-authors and Agile leaders, including Robert “Uncle Bob” Martin, Woody Zuill, Tim Ottinger, and Allen Holub.

I've had great conversations with engineers behind React, Angular, Vue, Solid, Svelte, Lit, Astro, 11ty, and W3C working groups, as well as others in the broader tech community.

For me, it's about actively seeking diverse perspectives from people shaping the field. I want to learn and contribute beyond what's possible in isolated bubbles. Thanks to that, I've learned more in the past few years than I might have learned in decades otherwise.
